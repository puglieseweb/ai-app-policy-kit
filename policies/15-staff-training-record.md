## Why this matters

A policy nobody has read protects nobody. A regulator, an insurer or a large customer will also ask for evidence that staff were told. Training does not need to be a course: half an hour with the policies and a few real examples is enough for most small teams.

## Who it covers

Everyone who works for [Business name], contractors included, from their first week.

## The rules

1. **New starters are trained in their first week,** before they get access to customer data.
2. **Everyone repeats it every 12 months,** and when a policy changes in a way that affects their work.
3. **Training covers the topics below,** with examples from this business.
4. **People with more access get more.** Anyone who writes or releases code, or administers systems, also covers documents 03, 04, 05, 12 and 14.
5. **Everyone confirms** they have read the policies that apply to them. Record it below.
6. **Practise.** Once a year, walk through a made-up incident as a team (document 06). Send a harmless test phishing email if you can.
7. **Nobody is punished for reporting.** People who report their own mistake quickly are thanked. A business that hears about problems early has fewer bad ones.

## What everyone covers

- [ ] Which AI tools are approved, and what must never go into them (document 01).
- [ ] What personal data is, and what we hold (document 02).
- [ ] Passwords, the password manager and two-step sign-in (document 03).
- [ ] Spotting phishing, fake invoices and voice or video impersonation, including AI-made fakes.
- [ ] Checking AI output before using it.
- [ ] What an incident is, and who to phone (document 06).
- [ ] What to do when a customer asks for their data or asks for it to be deleted (document 10).
- [ ] Locking devices, and reporting a lost one the same day.
